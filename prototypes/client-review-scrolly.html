Start here

$77,400 went out the door in August.

32 jobs at an average ticket of $2,419 — your highest average of the year, and about $120 a job above where you were running in the spring. That's the escalator, not a price rise your customers noticed.

The top bar is the whole of August. Everything below is where it went.

First cut

35.4 cents of every dollar is the job itself.

Copper, fixtures, fittings, the occasional sub. In July this was 37.9 cents. One month of quoting at today's material prices instead of March's moved it nearly two and a half points.

In plain terms: on the same $77,400 of work, quoting the old way would have left about $1,900 less on the table. That's one commitment, one month.
Second cut

Your crew takes the next 28 cents.

$21,800, and tracking plan within 2% all year. Payroll isn't the problem — worth saying out loud, because it's the first place everyone looks.

Third cut

Keeping the doors open cost $18,500.

Rent, insurance, trucks, fuel, software, phones, the bookkeeper. Plan was $15,650. Third month running over — and unlike the two cuts above, this one doesn't care how much work you sold. It goes out whether the phone rings or not.

In plain terms: that $2,850 overage is about one and a half jobs you worked in August purely to cover the gap. Three months of it is $8,500.
What's left

11.9 cents on the dollar — your best month since March.

$9,175 from August. The escalator did that, and it will keep doing it every month from here without anyone lifting a finger.

But eight months in you're at 9.8 cents against a plan of 13.2. One good month doesn't close that. The doors-open line and the collection lag do — and both are still open.

What the numbers are telling us

Three things. Not thirty.

There are eighteen numbers we track. Fifteen are fine. These are the three that aren't — and the third is really just the scoreboard for the first two.

Priority 1 Off plan Improving

You're still financing your customers for 48 days.

Down from 53 — the phone calls worked, and they worked fast. But the plan is 35, and those extra 13 days are the single biggest reason the bank balance doesn't feel like a business doing $830,000 a year.

Calling people is a cure. What we need next is a preventative, so this doesn't climb back to 53 the moment nobody's chasing.

In plain terms: $29,600 of your money is sitting in other people's accounts right now. That's about 12 completed jobs — or one service truck, paid cash.

One question for next month

If the $29,600 showed up tomorrow, what would you do with it?

I ask because the answer changes the advice. If it's the third truck, we should be talking about what that does to your crew plan and your insurance before you buy it. If it's paying down the line of credit, we should look at whether that's the cheapest debt you're carrying.
